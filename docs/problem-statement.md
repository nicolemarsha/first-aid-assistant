# Problem Statement

## Who is affected?
Untrained community members, non-expert first responders, and individuals providing initial emergency care during injury scenarios.

## What exactly is going wrong?
In high-stress emergency situations, non-experts struggle to assess injury severity accurately and often lack standard first aid tools or proper knowledge on how to safely improvise using available household items.

## Why does it matter?
Improper trauma severity assessment or unsafe improvisation of first aid tools can cause secondary physical harm, delayed care, or life-threatening complications before emergency medical services arrive.

## Problem statement (3 to 5 sentences)
Untrained community members and non-expert first responders may struggle to assess limb injuries and choose suitable first-aid steps during emergencie (Abraham et al., 2023). This problem becomes more difficult when standard first-aid supplies are unavailable and users do not know whether alternative materials are safe to use (Hewett Brumberg et al., 2024). Although computer vision can support injury assessment through image analysis, it may not provide enough information to guide users through the correct first-aid procedures (Clinical Validation of Computer Vision, 2023). Therefore, this project proposes an Emergency Injury Assessment with First Aid Recommender Support System that uses computer vision to support limb injury assessment and provide clear, safety-focused first-aid guidance.
